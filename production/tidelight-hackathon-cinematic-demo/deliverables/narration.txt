Markets do not wait for the bell. A company update, a policy shift, a new signal—each can ripple through tokenized markets while the headlines are quiet. Tidelight is built to help you follow that ripple with evidence, context, and a decision that stays yours.

Start with the market map. This is Bitget’s public spot universe, including tokenized US equities. Quotes carry provider freshness and classification context. Tidelight is read-only here; browsing does not require an exchange key.

From an event, move to evidence. Tidelight’s research desk is designed around source material you bring: ask a question, inspect the captured claims beside supporting passages, and keep the source link close. The system is bounded by the evidence supplied. A useful answer should show what supports it, what might disagree, and what remains uncertain.

Agent Flow makes the handoffs visible. The journey connects event, evidence, exposure, scenario, and decision. Research can map a company story to available instruments and risk context. When gates do not agree, the right outcome can be to pause or hold. That is a research workflow—not an automatic recommendation.

The handoffs are the point. An event is the question, not the answer. Evidence gives it a traceable basis. Exposure asks which issuers and instruments may be affected. A scenario makes assumptions visible: what could happen, what could go the other way, and which new fact would change the view.

On the market map, token labels, provider timestamps, and session context distinguish a public quote from a complete investment picture. Tokenized instruments can differ in hours, liquidity, and issuer terms. A price is one observation; it does not prove that an asset can trade at that level or that a strategy can execute there.

For a market idea, Strategy Lab turns a rule into a reproducible historical replay. Choose an instrument, interval, and deterministic strategy; review the costs, data window, holdout, and trade record. The published validation is candid: its tested baseline underperformed buy and hold across five markets in that snapshot. It is an experiment to inspect, not a claim of alpha. The hackathon’s Alpha Factory bar asks for at least sixty calendar days with thirty out of sample, so that qualification matters.

Strategy Lab replays deterministic rules over completed public candles, with a chronological holdout and explicit fee and slippage assumptions. Tidelight records the window and outputs so a result can be examined and repeated. A replay helps test a rule; one short sample does not prove durable performance.

Nightwatch carries the same discipline into paper mode. It reviews completed Bitget candles, records why it proposes or declines an action, and applies explicit account and loss limits before a simulated fill. Its ledger is private to a signed-in workspace. This public view is logged out; no private decisions or fills are being shown here. No live exchange orders are sent.

Nightwatch reviews completed candles and paper-account context, then records rationale, confidence, risks, and invalidation. A hard risk rule can reject a proposal, and a hold is a valid outcome. Fees and slippage are estimated; simulated fills are not exchange fills. A signed-in workspace is needed to show its private ledger.

The trading pages expose distinct review paths, including simulated workflows. This walkthrough does not connect an exchange account, submit a live order, or suggest that a paper result is ready for execution. Human judgment remains at the center: inspect the source, understand the market context, test the assumption, and decide whether the question deserves another look.

Event. Evidence. Exposure. Scenario. Decision. Tidelight brings the pieces into one calmer market desk—so when the tide turns, you can see what changed, check the reasoning, and choose your next step with your eyes open.
